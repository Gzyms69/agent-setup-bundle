# Marketing Copywriting Skill

This skill provides conversion copywriting guidelines to write copy that is clear, compelling, and drives customer action.

---

## 1. Copywriting Core Principles

*   **Clarity Over Cleverness:** If forced to choose between a creative pun or a clear statement, choose the clear one. The user must understand your product in 3 seconds.
*   **Benefits Over Features:**
    *   *Feature (What it does):* "Our tool has a drag-and-drop report builder."
    *   *Benefit (Outcome for user):* "Build professional dashboard reports in 2 minutes, no design skills needed."
*   **Specificity Over Vagueness:** Replace vague buzzwords with concrete numbers:
    *   *Vague:* "Save hours on your workflow."
    *   *Specific:* "Reduce your weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes."
*   **Customer Language:** Mirror the Voice-of-Customer (VoC) using terms actual customers write in support tickets, reviews, or interviews. Avoid corporate jargon.
*   **One Idea Per Section:** Keep copy sections focused on a single key message to ensure layout readability.

---

## 2. Writing Style Rules & Eliminating "AI-isms"

AI-generated writing has recognizable robotic patterns. Cleanse copy of the following:
1.  **Simple over complex:** Use "help" not "facilitate," "use" not "utilize," "start" not "commence."
2.  **Outcome over process:** Write in the active voice ("We write reports" instead of "Reports are generated by our systems").
3.  **Qualifier Removal:** Strip helper adverbs like "really," "very," "almost," "simply."
4.  **Buzzword Bans:** Ban terms like "streamline," "optimize," "innovative," "game-changer," "cutting-edge," "unlock," "elevate," "foster."
5.  **Exclamation Limit:** Do not use exclamation points in body copy or headers. Confidence is communicated through specificity, not punctuation.

## 3. Headline & CTA Formulas

### Headline Scoping
Your headline is the most important sentence on the page. Write 5-10 candidates, evaluate them, and present the top 2-3 to the user using these formulas:
*   **Formula 1 (Outcome without pain):** `[Achieve desired outcome] without [primary pain point]`
    *   *Example:* "Ship frontend dashboards in minutes, not sprints."
*   **Formula 2 (Targeted Category):** `The [product category] built for [ideal customer audience]`
    *   *Example:* "The analytics platform built for security engineers."
*   **Formula 3 (Bold Outcome):** `Never [unpleasant event] again`
    *   *Example:* "Never manually reconcile billing records again."

### Call-to-Action (CTA) Copy
Avoid weak verbs (Submit, Sign Up, Click Here, Get Started). Use action-oriented, value-conveying copy:
*   **Formula:** `[Action Verb] + [What They Get] + [Qualifier if helpful]`
*   **Examples:**
    *   "Start My Free 14-Day Trial"
    *   "Get the SEO Checklist"
    *   "See Pricing for My Team"

## 4. Page Structure Frameworks

### Homepage Copy
*   Lead with the broadest value proposition.
*   Address multiple user personas by providing clear navigation paths.
*   Incorporate social proof logos, core benefits (3 items), a "How It Works" step guide, FAQs, and a final CTA.

### Landing Page Copy
*   Focus on a single message and a single, unified CTA.
*   Match the headline copy exactly to the ad source or traffic referral context.
*   Maintain layout simplicity to prevent user distractions.

### Pricing Page Copy
*   Highlight the recommended or most popular plan clearly.
*   Solve the "which plan is right for me?" anxiety using clear audience tags.
*   Provide transparent feature tables and security guarantee callouts.

---

## 5. Output Format Standard

When delivering copywriting recommendations, output in this structure:

1.  **Bottom Line First:** Present the primary copy recommendation directly.
2.  **Alternatives List:** Provide 2-3 headline or CTA alternatives:
    *   *Option A (Formula type):* [copy text] - [rationale]
    *   *Option B (Formula type):* [copy text] - [rationale]
3.  **Copy Annotations:** For each text block, explain the conversion copywriting principles applied.
4.  **Confidence Tagging:**
    *    *Strong recommendation* (best practices, high conversion probability).
    *    *Test this* (worth testing in A/B variants).
    *    *Needs proof* (requires testimonials or metrics to validate the claim).

---

## 6. Anti-Rationalization Table

| Rationalization | Correction |
| :--- | :--- |
| "The client wants this buzzword" | **BLOCKED:** Educate on alternatives, propose A/B test. Buzzwords kill conversion by obscuring value. |
| "This headline is good enough" | **BLOCKED:** Always provide 3 headline variants. The first idea is rarely the best performing. |
| "SEO requires keyword stuffing" | **BLOCKED:** Modern SEO penalizes stuffing. Write for humans first, then naturally weave in semantic keywords. |
| "The copy is too short" | **BLOCKED:** Concise copy converts better than verbose copy. Every word must fight for its place. |
| "Exclamation marks add energy" | **BLOCKED:** Limit to 1 per page maximum. Energy comes from strong verbs and compelling offers, not punctuation. |

## 7. Red Flags
*   Copy that explains *how* the product works before explaining *why* the user should care.
*   Passive voice ("Mistakes were made" vs "We made a mistake").
*   Generic CTAs like "Submit" or "Click Here".
*   Walls of unbroken text.
*   Focusing entirely on the company instead of the customer (too many "we" statements, not enough "you" statements).

## 8. Verification Gates
1.  **Does the headline clearly state what the product is and who it is for?**
2.  **Are benefits highlighted instead of just features?**
3.  **Is the copy free of banned buzzwords and unnecessary qualifiers?**
4.  **Is there exactly one primary Call to Action (CTA) on the landing page?**
